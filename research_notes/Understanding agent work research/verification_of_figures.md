## 1. Do the report's headline figures match the primary sources?

### Takeaway

Almost every number in the report is correct as a number. Thirty-odd figures were confirmed in full text, including all figures previously marked "abstract only" for PyCG, the ESEM architecture study, Kluger and DeNisi, Schroeder, Macnamara, Ghoniem, Parnin, LaToza and Myers, the alert-override review, Tang et al. and the CMU Cursor study. The errors are in descriptions around the numbers: the Australian GP study is not a controlled trial, the Anthropic sample is not "mostly junior" by the paper's own table, one METR claim is not in the cited post, one citation link points to the wrong paper, and the Kazemitabaar second study found no learning gain at all.

### Inferences

- The first pass's numbers were transcribed accurately even when taken from abstracts. Its weak point was the description of study design and population when the abstract did not state them (the GP study, the Anthropic sample, the Ghoniem sizes, the PyCG benchmark).
- Three of the report's supports for its own design choices are weaker in full text than in abstract: the Kazemitabaar technique showed no learning gain in its second study; the concept-map contrast (0.72 vs 0.43) is between-study; the "above twenty nodes" threshold is the smallest size tested.

- The Balepur extension-task result cuts against the simplest reading of the comprehension finding: lower measured comprehension did not produce a significantly worse result when extending the code without the agent.
