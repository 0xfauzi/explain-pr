Firsthand Product summary. Nothing built yet. 7 Nov 2026.

Open source. Working name [unverified: trademark, registries]

Build at agent speed. Become a much better engineer while you do it.

Firsthand installs into the coding agent you already use. At a few natural breaks, it asks you to do the part of the thinking that builds skill: predict, name a cause, write the key line, explain, or review a pull request first. Days later it brings the idea back in a new form. Drills made from your own history, and tasks in repos you have never opened, give you practice with the agent off.

Why: in a study of people learning a new library, those who let AI write the code scored 50% on a quiz against 67% for those who did not, with the biggest gap on debugging. The AI users who kept their learning asked conceptual questions or checked their understanding (Shen and Tamkin 2026, arxiv.org/html/2601.20245v2). Firsthand builds that habit into the agent.

The design follows the research on how people learn: think first, retrieve later, space it out, vary the context, practise what erodes. Each choice below cites the study behind it.

The product

Three parts

1

In the build

Five moves at natural breaks, inside your agent. Never blocks the next step. One-word skip on every question, and "just tell me" always works.

2

Away from the build

At most 3 reviews a day, in new forms. Drills when you want them: trace your old code, replay a real bug with the agent off, check past diffs, or take a task in a repo you have never opened.

3

The map

A private record in a git repo on your machine, outside any project. Shows what you have done without the agent, how it was graded, and how long ago. A level goes down when you forget.

Why this design

Each choice follows a finding

Asks you to think first, at a few chosen pointsGenerating an answer beats reading one, d = 0.40 (Bertsch 2007). Stating the step before seeing AI code aligned felt and real ability best (Kazemitabaar 2025, arxiv).
Brings ideas back days later, as free recallPractice testing and spacing are the two highest-utility techniques (Dunlosky 2013). Daily spaced questions at work still showed an effect two years on (Kerfoot 2009, pubmed).
Spends practice on debugging and checking agent outputDebugging had the largest gap in Shen and Tamkin. Watching automation is not practice, and the thinking part decays first (Bainbridge 1983; Casner 2014).
Asks in new forms, topic name hidden, across projectsTransfer fails when nothing cues the principle. Comparing two cases nearly tripled its use (Gentner, Loewenstein and Thompson 2003).
Counts only delayed answers, without helpDoing well in the moment is not learning (Soderstrom and Bjork 2015, pubmed). People cannot feel which way taught them more (Deslauriers 2019).
Silent on what you already knowHelp for a novice can hurt someone who knows the topic (Kalyuga 2007). So it adapts per idea, not per person.
No streaks, points or badgesLeaderboards and badges lowered motivation and exam scores over 16 weeks (Hanus and Fox 2015).

What a level means

Only delayed answers, without help, on new questions, count. Two right answers on different items move a level up. One miss moves it down a step.

Not yet
The start, or one step down after a delayed miss or "don't know".
Recalled
Two right delayed answers, on two items, on different days.
Applied
Two right on new instances: another file or project, topic name hidden.
Held
Right at a longer gap, plus one other kind: a Fresh repo task, a confirmed finding, or real work.
Met is a separate list, not a level: ideas you explained, wrote a line for, or answered with help or in the moment.

Architecture

One engine on your computer. Many ways in.

  • Tiers stack. Every surface has T0. Each tier above it adds something. The engine detects what is there and always returns complete text.
  • The pad is how answers count. A chat answer passes through the agent, so it counts as Met. An answer typed in the pad never reaches the agent or its logs.
  • Sealed keys. The answer key's hash is committed before you see the question, and released only after your answer is locked.
  • Grading by running code first. Tests decide wherever they can. Open answers get model feedback with a quote for every point.
  • Code you did not write runs in a container with no network, or not at all. Replay and Fresh repo need one.
  • No server, no telemetry. Answers typed in the agent's chat are part of that chat, and the agent's company sees them. The install says so. Hooks never approve anything.

Keeping the agents accurate

Wrong grades are rare, visible and cheap

No experiment. The design checks the agents at every step instead.

keys from the code

A prediction's key is the real output of running the code. Every point in an explain key cites a file and line, and the engine checks that the line says it.

answered blind first

Before a question is asked, a second agent answers it from the code alone. If it does not match the key, the question is dropped.

grades show evidence

Each point must quote your answer. Two passes must agree, or the level does not move. You see the quotes and the code, and can dispute.

One wrong grade cannot move a level: a level goes up only after two right delayed answers on different days. A set of answers graded by people runs in CI before any grader change ships.
